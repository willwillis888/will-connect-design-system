Will Connect — Voice and Tone
Version 1.0 — April 2026
Apply this to every word produced for Will Connect.
If a draft conflicts with this file — rewrite the draft.
---
CORE IDENTITY
Write like a founder who has thought more clearly about this problem
than anyone else in the market.
Not a startup trying to impress.
Not performing expertise.
Grounded, present, genuinely in service of the reader.
Human before professional.
The writing sounds like a real person who knows their craft —
warmth comes through in specificity, not in adjectives.
---
THE SEVEN PRINCIPLES
These are the foundation. Every piece of writing reflects at least one.
1. Learner-first, ego-last
The reader's reality comes before credentials.
Put their problem before the product. Always.
The emotional state of the reader is the first variable — not the content.
2. Three-part clarity
Where they are winning. Where they are stuck. What they do next.
Always that progression. Never skip to the solution.
This framework informs how Will Connect's value is described —
not a list of capabilities, a clear progression.
3. Vulnerability plus intention equals authority
Human and grounded at the same time.
Nothing to prove and everything to offer.
Showing up this way is what opens people up — in all the most professional ways.
4. Reverse engineer first
Start from the end goal. Work back to the real source of the breakdown.
The real problem is almost always simpler than the story people tell about it.
This diagnostic instinct should read as: clear, layered, patient.
5. Fierce curiosity — not ambition, not aggression
Attentiveness. Genuine interest in the problem.
Intensity that reads as care, not pressure.
Curiosity, not ambition, is the tone.
6. Systems thinker
Organised and intentional without being clinical.
Finds order in cycles, patterns, and structure.
Brings that quality to learning design, product thinking, and copy alike.
7. The real problem is buried
Go layers deeper than the surface explanation.
Avoid surface-level answers.
The core of the breakdown is typically different from the reasons first offered.
Front-loading that digging saves time — and that patience should read in the writing.
---
TONE WORDS
Clear. Authoritative. Grounded. Precise. Warm. Invitational.
Outcome-focused. Emotionally aware. Service-oriented.
Not: performative, salesy, confrontational, verbose,
clever for its own sake, scene-setting, corporate, generic.
---
WHAT IT SOUNDS LIKE
"Good learning design starts with one question:
what does this person actually need to do differently?"
"The real problem is almost never where people think it is."
"Emotional buy-in is the part most training skips —
and it is usually why training does not stick."
"I care most about the moment something lands.
That is what I design for."
"I drop my agenda and read the person in front of me.
Everything else follows from that."
"Vulnerability plus clear intention is what brings the authority.
It lets people let their guard down in all the most professional ways."
---
WHAT IT NEVER SOUNDS LIKE
"At the intersection of instructional design and platform implementation..."
"I am passionate about leveraging my expertise to drive transformative outcomes..."
"With a holistic approach, I seamlessly align stakeholder needs with product goals..."
Any opening that sets a scene instead of making a point.
Any sentence that describes what Will Connect does without saying what changes.
Any writing that lists capabilities without showing what they produce.
---
THE READER TEST
The reader should finish and think:
"This person understands my problem more precisely than I do.
I want to have this conversation."
Not: "This looks impressive."
Not: "I should sign up for a free trial."
Not: "This is another analytics tool."
---
PLATFORM TONE ADJUSTMENTS
Landing page (primary context)
Direct. Narrative. Each section earns the next.
No scene-setting. First sentence does the work.
Reads like someone who has already earned the right to say this.
X content
Short. Punchy. Opinion-led. No hashtag spam.
Uses the locked opener for Will Connect content.
Every post passes: Is this creator-facing? Does it sound human? Would a creator save this?
Outreach / DMs
Written to a person, not an audience.
Opens with something real and specific to them.
Never a pitch disguised as a question.
Maximum three short paragraphs. Direct. Human. Done.
Pitch materials
Fragments over sentences. Visual anchors over paragraphs.
Business owners do a 2-second visual scan — design for that.
30–40% fewer words than feels comfortable.
