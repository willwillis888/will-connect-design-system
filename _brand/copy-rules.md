Will Connect — Copy Rules
Version 1.0 — April 2026
These are absolute. No exceptions. No context overrides them.
If a draft breaks any of these rules — rewrite it before output.
---
ABSOLUTE PROHIBITIONS
Punctuation
No em dashes — use a comma, full stop, or restructure the sentence
No exclamation marks anywhere in any Will Connect copy
No ellipses used for dramatic effect
Words and phrases — never use
platform → always use "system"
AI-powered → never frame Will Connect with AI language
revolutionary / game-changing / next-level / cutting-edge / innovative
intersection (as in "at the intersection of...")
leverage (as a verb)
synergy
unlock
journey
transformative
seamlessly
holistic
robust (unless describing a specific framework)
deep dive
passionate about (especially as a sentence opener)
I help (as a framing device)
execution gap intelligence → internal use only, never public-facing
Structure
No bullet points with icons in narrative sections
No passive construction where active works
→ "I led the transition" not "the transition was led"
No three consecutive sentences starting with "I"
No scene-setting before making a point
→ First sentence does the work. Always.
No opening that reads like a newsletter lede or editorial intro
No sentence that describes what Will Connect does without saying what changes
---
WHAT ALWAYS HAPPENS
Specificity over vagueness
"6 weeks, one module" not "a short focused engagement"
"6 staff, 2 weeks" not "a small team in a short timeframe"
Concrete version always beats the vague version
If you cannot be specific — that is a signal to investigate, not to generalise
Short sentences
They land harder.
Use them.
Steve's natural speech has rhythm and punch — writing should too.
Active voice
"Will Connect maps where reasoning breaks down"
Not "reasoning breakdowns are mapped by Will Connect"
Contractions
Allowed and appropriate in landing page and content contexts
"It doesn't stick" is fine
"It does not stick" for more formal contexts
Match the formality of the surface
Outcome-pointing
Every sentence should point toward a result, a behaviour, or a feeling
Not just describe activity
"What changes at the end of this?" — that is the question every sentence answers
---
PUBLIC vs INTERNAL LANGUAGE
Internal only	Public-facing equivalent
Execution Gap Intelligence	Applied reasoning diagnostic
EGI	Never used publicly
CEP (Community Execution Profile)	Community performance view / cohort dashboard
Struggling / Developing / Advanced / Elite	Performance bands (describe, don't label publicly)
---
THE COPY TEST
Before any copy is finalised, run it through:
Does it serve one of the three core questions?
(How well are members executing? Where struggling? What to do?)
Does the first sentence do actual work — or does it set a scene?
Would a trading course creator read this and think
"this person understands my problem more precisely than I do"?
Is there an em dash, exclamation mark, or prohibited word anywhere?
Can anything be made more specific?
If the answer to 4 is yes or 5 is yes — rewrite before output.
